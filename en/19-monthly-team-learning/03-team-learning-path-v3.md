- Course description:

Embodied AI is very trendy. From Mobile ALOHA to Pi0 and OpenVLA, SOTA models are updated almost every week. However, many people want to get involved but get stuck at the first step:

🤯 The materials are too fragmented; unsure where to start learning from?

🤯 The threshold is too high; the mathematical derivations and physics engine are overwhelming?

🤯 Can't run, and the environment configuration is so complicated that it makes you doubt everything?

Don’t panic! This beginner-friendly tutorial Every-Embodied: Zero to Hero, initiated by Datawhale and refined by the open-source community, is now officially open source!🌟 Our focus is on: easy for beginners, reproducible in engineering, and adaptable to cutting-edge technologies. It takes you from a single line of Python code to gradually installing a “brain” for the robot!🧠

The path for this version remains unchanged with Task01 as originally scheduled, allowing everyone to continue using the existing teaching assistant schedules and check-in links; adjustments will begin from Task02. The overall strategy is: first complete basic general knowledge, then enter different fields based on interests. The latter part is divided into three categories: **manipulation world model and world model**, **navigation model and drone navigation**, and **applied practice and system deployment**. Students who are interested in robotic arm manipulation, grasping, VLA, ACT, Pi0, SmolVLA, OpenVLA, and world model can enter the manipulation world model and world model branch; those who prefer navigation, VLN, scene understanding, drone navigation, and space task planning can enter the navigation model and drone navigation branch; those aiming to develop development boards, teleoperation, simulation tools, and multi-modal demos can enter the applied practice and system deployment branch.

​

- Target audience: Students, engineers, and researchers interested in embodied AI; those with a foundation in Python and basic mathematical knowledge. Beginners in Python / AI developers: Those who want to systematically transition into embodied AI fields; university students: Those needing course projects or final projects, or those aiming to quickly develop the ability to reproduce research results; hardware/robotics enthusiasts: Those seeking to establish a complete closed loop from physical machine control to Sim2Real deployment.
